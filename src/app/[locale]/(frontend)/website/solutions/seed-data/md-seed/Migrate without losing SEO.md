## **The Biggest Concerns Business Leaders Have About Website Migrations**

### **Losing Years of Rankings and Organic Traffic**

An established website has accumulated valuable pages, links, relevance and search history. If these signals are removed or redirected incorrectly, a visually successful launch can create a serious commercial decline.

### **Discovering SEO Problems Only After Launch**

Many migration issues begin during planning, design and development. When SEO is invited only for the final check, important decisions may already be built into templates, navigation, URLs and content, making them more expensive and difficult to correct.

### **Redirecting Thousands of URLs Incorrectly**

Incomplete mappings, broad homepage redirects, chains and irrelevant destinations can waste existing authority and create a poor customer experience. Large and multilingual websites require careful URL-level decisions and controlled implementation.

### **Launching a Website Search Engines Cannot Access**

Staging directives, robots rules, canonical tags, JavaScript rendering, authentication and indexation settings can accidentally follow the new website into production. One overlooked configuration can prevent important pages from being discovered or indexed.

### **Different Teams Assuming Someone Else Owns SEO**

Migrations involve developers, designers, content teams, analytics specialists, agencies and leadership. Without defined responsibilities, essential SEO requirements can be delayed, misunderstood or lost between teams.

### **Missing the Planned Launch Date**

SEO should reduce launch risk without becoming an unstructured list of late requests. We define requirements early, prioritise critical items and separate genuine launch blockers from improvements that can safely follow later.

### **Launching Successfully but Losing Revenue Weeks Later**

Not every problem is visible on launch day. Search engines need time to crawl and process the new website, while redirect, indexation and ranking issues may emerge gradually. Post-launch monitoring is essential for identifying and correcting them quickly.

## **How We Help You Migrate Without Losing SEO?**

The migration plan depends on what is changing, the size and value of the website, the platform, the number of markets and the project stage at which we become involved.

### **Organic Performance Baseline**

We record the existing website’s traffic, rankings, conversions, indexation, backlinks and strongest landing pages. This establishes what needs protection and provides a reliable point of comparison after launch.

### **Historical Website Crawl and URL Inventory**

We create an inventory using crawl data, sitemaps, analytics, search-engine reports, backlinks and other available sources. This reduces the chance that valuable pages are missed simply because they are no longer linked prominently.

### **New Website Architecture Review**

We assess navigation, hierarchy, categories, services, international sections and internal-link pathways. The new structure should improve the experience without making important content harder for users and search engines to reach.

### **Content Mapping and Preservation**

We compare existing and planned pages to determine what should remain, move, merge, improve or be removed. Valuable content and search intent should not disappear because the new design uses shorter templates or different page types.

### **URL Mapping and Redirect Strategy**

We map old URLs to the most relevant new destinations and define the correct redirect rules. We avoid broad, misleading mappings and test for missing URLs, chains, loops, errors and redirects that do not preserve the original page’s purpose.

### **Staging Website Technical Review**

We crawl and test the staging environment for indexation controls, canonicals, status codes, internal links, sitemaps, robots directives, rendering, structured data, mobile accessibility and performance. We compare templates and page groups at scale.

### **International and Multilingual Migration**

For websites operating across countries and languages, we review hreflang, regional URLs, canonicals, sitemaps, navigation and local content. Market-specific mappings help prevent one language or country version from replacing another incorrectly.

### **Analytics and Tracking Continuity**

We coordinate with analytics specialists to protect essential tracking, conversion events and reporting continuity. Without a consistent measurement setup, real SEO changes can be confused with missing or altered data.

### **Pre-Launch Quality Assurance**

Before launch, we recheck critical requirements, redirect readiness, indexation controls, metadata, content, tracking and known blockers. We provide a prioritised view of what must be resolved and what can safely follow after launch.

### **Launch-Day SEO Checks**

We verify that the live website is accessible, redirects work, important pages return the correct status, canonical and robots settings are appropriate, tracking is active and search engines can reach the intended content.

### **Post-Launch Monitoring and Issue Response**

We monitor crawling, indexation, traffic, rankings, conversions, errors and affected page groups. Unexpected changes are investigated quickly, while normal processing fluctuations are separated from issues requiring intervention.

### **Migration Implementation Support**

Where access and scope allow, our technical and content specialists can implement fixes directly. For code, infrastructure or platform work owned by another team, we prepare actionable requirements, discuss constraints and validate the released solution.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

### **What types of website migration do you support?**

We can support redesigns, CMS and platform changes, domain moves, HTTP to HTTPS migrations, URL restructuring, website consolidation, international expansion and combinations of these changes. The scope depends on the size, complexity and project stage.

### **When should SEO become involved in a migration?**

As early as possible. SEO input is most valuable while architecture, templates, content and URL decisions can still be influenced. Waiting until just before launch can make important changes more expensive or impossible within the planned timeline.

### **Can you guarantee that we will not lose any traffic?**

No responsible agency can guarantee completely unchanged traffic or rankings. Search engines need time to process a new website, and some fluctuation may occur. We reduce avoidable risk, protect valuable signals and monitor closely so genuine issues can be identified and corrected.

### **Do all old URLs need redirects?**

Every valuable or discoverable old URL should be assessed, but not every page should automatically redirect to the homepage. The destination should be genuinely relevant. Some URLs may return another appropriate status when no useful replacement exists.

### **Can you create the redirect map?**

Yes. We build URL inventories from several available sources, map old pages to suitable new destinations and test the redirect implementation for missing URLs, chains, loops, errors and irrelevant mappings.

### **Do you review the staging website?**

Yes. Staging review is a central part of the process. We crawl templates and page groups, compare them with the existing website and test technical, content, internal-link and international signals before launch.

### **Can you implement the migration recommendations?**

Yes, where we have the required access and the change falls within our expertise. We can implement many SEO and content fixes directly. For specialist development or infrastructure work, we provide precise requirements and validate the completed implementation.

### **Can you work with our existing web-development agency?**

Yes. We regularly collaborate with internal and external technical teams. We respect project ownership, explain why requirements matter, discuss platform limitations and remain available while tasks are implemented and tested.

### **Can you support a multilingual or international migration?**

Yes. We can review country and language architecture, hreflang, canonicals, sitemaps, content mapping and redirects across international websites. Complex migrations require market-level testing because global reports can hide local issues.

### **What should be monitored after launch?**

We monitor crawling, indexation, status codes, redirects, sitemaps, rankings, organic traffic, conversions and the performance of priority page groups and markets. The exact framework depends on what changed during the migration.

### **How long should post-launch monitoring continue?**

The required period depends on website size, crawl frequency, migration complexity and the speed at which search engines process the changes. Critical checks begin immediately, while meaningful stabilisation may require monitoring across several weeks or months.

### **What if our new website has already launched and traffic has fallen?**

We can begin with a migration recovery investigation. We compare the old and new websites, reconstruct the launch timeline, segment the losses and identify the technical, content or structural changes most likely to have affected performance.

### **Should we keep all content from the old website?**

Not necessarily. Weak, duplicated or obsolete content can be improved, consolidated or removed, but the decision should consider rankings, traffic, backlinks, search intent and commercial value. Design preference alone should not determine what disappears.

### **Can a domain change preserve existing SEO value?**

A carefully managed domain migration can transfer many existing signals, but it requires accurate redirects, consistent technical implementation, verification and monitoring. Changing the domain adds risk and should have a clear business reason.
